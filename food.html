Numbers need context.

This dataset represents specifically named raw foods. Nutrient values vary by variety, ripeness, preparation and source.

Serving basis

Percentage bars
FDA Daily Values for adults and children aged 4 years and older—not a personal prescription.

How a Daily Value bar works

Divide the nutrient amount in the selected serving by its Daily Value, then multiply by 100. For example, 18 mg of vitamin C ÷ 90 mg × 100 = 20% DV.

A bar describes a contribution to a reference value. It is not the percentage of the food made of that nutrient, or the fraction your body absorbs. Our bars stop visually at 100%; their labels can exceed 100%, as with guava.

Compare like with like

Start with the same weight and preparation state. A 100 g raw-food record does not describe a bowl of cooked food: cooking changes water content, serving volume and some nutrient retention.

An unreported value means “not available,” not automatically zero. A nutrient listed in a food is also different from the amount your body absorbs. This catalog is a selected dataset, not a complete list of every food or mineral.

Nutrient grams and energy are different

A gram measures mass; a kilocalorie measures energy. Fat supplies about 9 kcal per gram. An energy chart must use the appropriate energy factor for each macronutrient, rather than treating every gram as an equal share of energy.

Our comparison shows grams and kilocalories separately. Minerals and vitamins do not belong in a macronutrient energy calculation.
